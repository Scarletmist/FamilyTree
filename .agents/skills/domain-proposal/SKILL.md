# Domain procedure proposal

1. Read the task, domain lens, original sources and baseline behavior. Identify the practitioner decision, required artifact, controlling evidence, cross-field consistency and omitted domain checks.
2. Distinguish a general execution mistake from a domain reasoning gap. Leave generic timeout, tool sequencing and mechanical extraction rules to INV unless needed to make a domain operation explicit.
3. Encode operations the solver can apply and verify, such as reconcile identities, check coordinate frames, compare alternatives, calculate a quantity or trace a dependency. State inputs, expected result, conflicts and uncertainty. Do not invent standards, citations, units or task facts.
4. Prefer triggered domain skills and bundled helpers over long always-on subject-matter prose. A verified workspace fact may be recorded with its exact evidence and task scope in the candidate's task-local memory. Do not promote that fact as a universal constant for later tasks.
5. Produce actual candidate files and a `harness/templates/proposal.md` record. Identify sources, applicability, expected behavioral change, risks and falsifying probe. Reverify facts copied from earlier experience against current inputs.
6. Preserve independence from peer proposals in parallel mode. Use the declared current checkpoint in sequential mode. If evidence supports no domain change, KEEP_CURRENT with the exact inherited revision.
7. Hand off to `harness-surface-validation` and `candidate-probing`. A domain rule earns adoption through observed behavior and usable output, not because its text sounds expert.
